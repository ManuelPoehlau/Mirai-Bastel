## Task 1 — radial fit of the custom iris (`out/iris_custom.json`), approved

**Problem (measured on Manu's texture, the "unrefracted" iris from François et al. 2009, 512² RGBA):** in shares of
half the image width, its pupil edge is at ≈ **0.22** and its iris ends at ≈ **0.90** (white outside); the lab's
geometry has the pupil edge at **0.268**, the visible limbus at **0.819** (P1) and the chamber angle at **0.779**
(P1b). So P1b paints the image's inner iris ring 0.22–0.268 black (the geometric pupil), and the image's outer band
incl. its own limbal ring is hidden. Method used: mean luminance per 1 % radius ring; pupil = first ring > 0.15,
outer edge = first ring > 0.85 beyond 0.5 (the image's background is white).

**Spec (agreed in chat):**
- Optional sidecar `out/iris_custom.json`, e.g. `{"pupil": 0.22, "limbus": 0.90}` = where the image's pupil edge and
  iris edge lie, as shares of half the image width. Read-only, validated (0 < pupil < limbus ≤ 1, numbers); invalid →
  ignored with the reason shown (HUD / console / probe line, as `IrisSource.describe`). No sidecar → exactly today's
  mapping (P1 pixel tests must stay green).
- Radial remap, piecewise linear in the radius share s = r / `IRIS_RADIUS` of the lab: `[0, s_pupil_lab] → [0,
  pupil]`, `[s_pupil_lab, s_limbus_lab] → [pupil, limbus]`, beyond → continue to the image edge. The angle is kept.
  This is the standard pupil-dilation remap; a later pupil-size control can reuse it.
- **Open choice for Manu (ask, with a picture):** map the image's iris edge to the visible limbus 0.819 (P1's edge; in
  P1b the band 0.779–0.819 then lies under limbal tissue) or to the chamber angle 0.779 (P1b shows the whole image,
  P1 shows white background in that band). It interacts with Task 2.
- Optional helper: estimate the two shares from the image with the profile method above and *print a suggestion*;
  only the JSON file sets them.
- **Where the mapping lives today (both must change together):**
  - P1: UVs baked into the iris mesh (`eye_geometry.iris_mesh` → `iris_uv`, set at mesh build; the textured
    production program reads them). Apply the remap to the mesh UVs when the sidecar is present (e.g. pass a mapping
    into `iris_mesh`, or rewrite the face UVs in the scene builders via `Mesh.set_face_uvs` before the first sync).
    Pupil-tube / floor faces map to the image centre and stay so.
  - P1b: `refraction_shaders.CORNEA_FRAGMENT_BODY` computes `vec2(0.5) + hit.xy / (2.0 * u_iris_radius)`. Add the same
    remap as a GLSL function with a NumPy twin and a parity case (`refraction_parity.py`). Keep sampling before any
    branch (implicit derivatives).
  - The CPU model (`cornea_optics.iris_albedo_image`, `optics_report`) keeps the procedural iris on purpose.
- **Tests:** identity without sidecar (existing pixel tests); JSON validation cases (headless); a GL test with a
  marker image (a ring painted at the image's pupil share) that lands on the geometric pupil edge in P1 and P1b; the
  sidecar and image unchanged afterwards.

## Task 2 — soft limbus transition: plan first, then build

**What exists:**
- P1: a hard seam. The sclera mesh is open at the limbus ring (r 5.88 mm), the cornea cap shares those vertices; no
  shading transition. The sclera's normal there points 29.3° off the eye's axis, the cornea's 48.9°: a **19.6° kink**,
  so highlights and reflections jump at the ring.
- P1b (V2 / V3): rays entering the cornea between the chamber angle (r 5.59 mm) and the rim end in **limbal tissue**,
  shaded opaque as a blend from a dark limbal colour (the procedural iris's outer colour) to the sclera albedo
  (`cornea_optics.limbus_weight`, smoothstep over the anterior entry radius). On Manu's sheet this reads as a thin
  bright rim at the edge, not as a soft grey-blue limbal zone.
- The sclera itself has no limbus-aware shading in any variant.

**Anatomy to aim at (to verify in the session, not from memory alone):** the limbus is a ~1–2 mm wide transition
where the clear cornea turns into the white sclera; it is semi-translucent, so the dark iris / chamber behind it shows
through as a soft grey-blue ring (the "limbal ring", stronger in young eyes); the sclera overlaps the cornea more at
top and bottom (the visible iris is slightly wider than high); the surface has a shallow groove (sulcus) rather than a
kink.

**Candidate variants for a P1c comparison (to be refined in the plan):**
- **L0** today (P1 seam / P1b tissue blend) as the reference.
- **L1 shading band on the sclera side:** within a band outside the ring, darken / tint the sclera toward the limbal
  colour as a function of the angle from the axis (cheap, no geometry).
- **L2 normal blend:** blend sclera and cornea normals across a band so reflections and highlights no longer jump at
  the 19.6° kink (shader-side, or geometric: a fillet / sulcus in the meshes — P1 meshes are lab-owned).
- **L3 translucent limbus:** sclera fragments within the band trace into the eye like the P1b cornea pass and mix the
  traced colour (iris edge / chamber) with the sclera by a thickness-driven opacity — the physically motivated one;
  needs the sclera pass to share P1b's ray code.
- Interaction with Task 1: with the radial fit, the image's own limbal ring may become visible; decide whether the
  texture or the shader owns the limbal darkening.

**Suggested order:** Task 1 (small, clear spec) → a short plan for Task 2 with the variants, measurements (GPU time on
the RPC, as before) and the views → Manu agrees → build L0–L3 as P1c in this lab, P1 / P1b unchanged.
